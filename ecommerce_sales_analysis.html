Online Retail Sales Analysis¶

Project Overview¶

Dataset: UCI Online Retail Dataset (2009-2011)
Goal: Practice end-to-end data analysis using Python to identify sales patterns and business insights

This analysis answers 4 key business questions to practice data analysis skills:

  1. Which products generate the most revenue?
  2. Who are our most valuable customers?
  3. What are our sales trends over time?
  4. What's our average order value?

Business Context¶

This dataset contains transactions from a UK-based online retailer selling unique gift items. The company serves both individual customers and wholesalers across multiple countries.

Key Metrics to Analyze:

  • Revenue drivers (products and customers)
  • Seasonal patterns and trends
  • Purchase behavior and order values

Tools Used: Python, pandas, matplotlib, Jupyter Notebook

df_clean = df.copy()

print("CLEANING DATA...")
print(f"Starting rows: {df_clean.shape[0]:,}")

print(f"After removing missing Customer ID: {df_clean.shape[0]:,}")

df_clean['Quantity'] = pd.to_numeric(df_clean['Quantity'], errors='coerce')
df_clean = df_clean[df_clean['Quantity'] > 0]
print(f"After removing returns: {df_clean.shape[0]:,}")

df_clean['Price'] = pd.to_numeric(df_clean['Price'], errors='coerce')
df_clean = df_clean[df_clean['Price'] > 0]
print(f"After removing invalid prices: {df_clean.shape[0]:,}")

df_clean['TotalRevenue'] = df_clean['Quantity'] * df_clean['Price']

df_clean['InvoiceDate'] = pd.to_datetime(df_clean['InvoiceDate'], format='mixed', dayfirst=True, errors='coerce')

df_clean['Year'] = df_clean['InvoiceDate'].dt.year
df_clean['Month'] = df_clean['InvoiceDate'].dt.month
df_clean['MonthName'] = df_clean['InvoiceDate'].dt.month_name()

order_value = df_clean.groupby('Invoice')['TotalRevenue'].sum()

print("="*60)
print("ORDER VALUE ANALYSIS")
print("="*60)
print(f"Total orders: {order_value.count():,}")
print(f"Average order value: ${order_value.mean():,.2f}")
print(f"Median order value: ${order_value.median():,.2f}")
print(f"Highest order: ${order_value.max():,.2f}")
print(f"Lowest order: ${order_value.min():,.2f}")
print(f"Standard deviation: ${order_value.std():,.2f}")

q25 = order_value.quantile(0.25)
q75 = order_value.quantile(0.75)
print(f"\n📊 Order Value Distribution:")
print(f"   • 25th percentile: ${q25:,.2f}")
print(f"   • 50th percentile (median): ${order_value.median():,.2f}")
print(f"   • 75th percentile: ${q75:,.2f}")

plt.figure(figsize=(12, 5))
order_value_filtered = order_value[order_value < 1000]
plt.hist(order_value_filtered, bins=50, color='purple', edgecolor='black', alpha=0.7)
plt.title('Distribution of Order Values (Under $1,000)', fontsize=16, fontweight='bold')
plt.xlabel('Order Value ($)', fontsize=12)
plt.ylabel('Number of Orders', fontsize=12)
plt.axvline(order_value.mean(), color='red', linestyle='--', linewidth=2, label=f'Average: ${order_value.mean():.2f}')
plt.axvline(order_value.median(), color='green', linestyle='--', linewidth=2, label=f'Median: ${order_value.median():.2f}')
plt.legend()
plt.grid(True, alpha=0.3, axis='y')
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

print(f"\n💡 KEY INSIGHTS:")
print(f"   → Average order value: ${order_value.mean():,.2f}")

Summary & Key Findings¶

Project Overview¶

Analyzed over 500K transactions from an online retail dataset to practice end‑to‑end data analysis using Python. The goal was to understand sales patterns and extract simple business insights.

Business Questions Answered¶

  1. Which products generate the most revenue?
    Identified the top-selling products by total revenue and highlighted a small set of items that drive a large share of sales.

  2. Who are our most valuable customers?
    Ranked customers by total spending and confirmed an 80/20 pattern where a minority of customers account for most revenue.

  3. What are our sales trends over time?
    Aggregated revenue by month to reveal clear seasonality, with certain months consistently outperforming others.

  4. What is our average order value?
    Calculated revenue per invoice, summarized the distribution (mean, median, quartiles), and showed room to increase basket size with upselling or bundling.

Skills Demonstrated¶

  • Data cleaning: Handling missing values, removing duplicates, and creating useful calculated columns (for example, total revenue per row).
  • Exploratory analysis: Using grouping, aggregation, and filtering to answer concrete business questions.
  • Visualization: Building line, bar, and histogram charts to communicate patterns in products, customers, and orders.
  • Business thinking: Turning raw metrics into simple recommendations, such as focusing on bestsellers, nurturing high‑value customers, and planning around seasonal peaks.

Tools and Learning Resources¶

  • Tools used: Python, pandas, matplotlib, Jupyter Notebook.
  • Dataset: Online retail transactions from a UK‑based e‑commerce business.
  • Learning approach: This project was built as a learning exercise with the help of online tutorials, documentation, and example notebooks, and then adapted and customized to fit these four questions.
